ZHENGW88 · PERSONAL LEVELING COMPANION

Play first. Sim the decisions.

A practical Frost path for your first Mage: solo quests, controlled pulls, occasional dungeons, and Ice Barrier at level 40.

My recommended starting plan, not a proven fastest route. Assumes normal talent points and no Legacy bonus points. Every allocation is checked against the October 30 extracted tree. Check launch tooltips before spending. No required respec. Current performance rankings remain experimental.

Your first session

  1. Set up the controls before optimizing. Put your core cast, instant finisher, wand Shoot, food/water and emergency abilities on comfortable binds as you learn them. Show target health, your mana and proc buffs.
  2. Follow your race's starter quest chain. Collect quests from a hub, group objectives in the same area, then turn them in together. Group up for a crowded named enemy; move to another nearby objective instead of waiting indefinitely. This is faction-neutral; no exact zone route is assumed.
  3. At levels 1–9, use the spells you actually have. Fireball is fine before Frostbolt. Once available, open with Frostbolt when its slow gives you useful distance. Use a wand to finish low-health enemies if you have one and can stand safely. Do not buy an expensive wand just because an old guide says it is mandatory.
  4. Keep the next pull safe. Start with one normal enemy near your level. Keep armor and Intellect buffs up as available, train useful new ranks when convenient, carry water and make bag space. Drink before a risky pull rather than after running out of mana.
  5. At level 10, follow the point order below. Your early practical goals are clean single-target pulls, few corpse runs and fewer unnecessary drinks—not a level-60 DPS score.
  6. Test once in the field. Time a short batch of comparable enemies, including drinking, looting and deaths. A change is useful if the whole batch improves without making pulls less reliable.

    Your in-game combat checklist
    1. Before pulling: check mana, nearby enemies and your escape direction. At level 40+, cast Ice Barrier before a planned pull when affordable. Do not refresh a healthy shield automatically just to imitate the sim.
    2. Open from range with Frostbolt. Cast again while you have room. If an enemy closes, use learned control tools such as Frost Nova, then create distance. Do not back into another pack.
    3. At level 20+, use Ice Lance for a real frozen opportunity. A slow is not a freeze. Use it on a rooted/frozen target or with Fingers of Frost, and respect its eight-second cooldown shared with Fire Blast. Spending Fire Blast can delay your next Lance. It is not your default spam spell.
    4. At levels 30–31, watch Fingers of Frost. Use an empowered Lance when ready, then keep casting useful spells with remaining charges. Do not stand idle for eight seconds waiting for a second Lance.
    5. From levels 35–39, Winter's Chill adds a Cone decision. If enemies are already safely within range, Cone can consume the stacks. Do not move into melee just to cash in stacks on an almost-dead enemy.
    6. From levels 46–47, spend Missile Barrage when stationary is safe. The proc makes the next Missiles channel free and faster. Avoid wasting a long channel on an enemy that needs only a small finisher.
    7. When a pull goes wrong: control or Polymorph a suitable extra enemy when available; use movement and defensive cooldowns early. Ice Block buys time but does not guarantee enemies disappear. Save Cold Snap for a useful defensive reset while learning.
    8. Start AoE gradually. Try two melee enemies with a clear escape path before larger packs. A high simulated pack DPS does not prove you can survive it. In dungeons, follow the tank's pull and crowd-control plan, and count travel/group-finding time when comparing leveling routes.
    

    Use the sim when a decision appears
    Good questions: Does this staff change casts-to-kill? Does more Intellect reduce recovery enough to matter? Is a short dungeon fight mana-limited? Does a result survive plausible changes to uncertain mechanics?
    Bad question: Which spec has the biggest default 120-second raid number, so I should level with it? The current model does not simulate enemy attacks, movement, root breaking, deaths or the practical value of Ice Block and Permafrost.
    1. Enter your actual stats below. For a Frost character on this path, use your displayed Intellect and Spirit, including the buffs you play with. If using Human, divide displayed Spirit by 1.10 before entering it because the sim adds that racial. Other modified/custom builds need pre-talent/pre-racial inputs as documented.
    2. Use one typical normal mob: same level, one target, your observed or estimated health, and a realistic casting distance. The 60-second setting is a timeout; damage stops when the target dies. All trials start at full mana, so this is not a continuous grinding simulation.
    3. Open the lab, run it, and inspect Frost. This loads the exact leveling allocation; the other schools still use research presets and are not survival-adjusted leveling alternatives.
    4. Click Download results or Share settings to keep a baseline. For an item comparison, duplicate that shared URL, change only the stats affected by the item, and run again with the same seed and target.
    5. Compare kill time, mana spent and full-refill recovery. Recovery uses the drinking rate you entered; it is not measured automatically. Treat tiny differences as inconclusive. Test the item in a small batch of real pulls before spending significant gold or respeccing.
    6. Repeat at useful checkpoints: a new spell rank, levels 20/30/40/47, a meaningful gear choice or a change from questing to dungeons. Do not interrupt every level to run sims.

    What to bring back from the game
    Your level, race/faction, exact talent allocation, stats with buffs, target level/health, and a short combat-log sample or timings from comparable pulls. Record total time for roughly 10 kills including drinking and deaths. If the sim disagrees, investigate ranks, costs and proc behavior; do not tune it merely to force the preferred spec to win.
